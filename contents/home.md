
I am passionate about developing technologies and using interdisciplinary approaches to study biology.

I obtained my Ph.D. in biological chemistry in Alice Ting's lab at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. My thesis work focused on developing a ratiometric tagging strategy combined with APEX proteomics to enable proteomic mapping of cellular regions of interest with a spatial specificity on the order of a few nanometers. I applied this to identify proteins that were involved interorganellar contact sites and identified a protein-protein interaction between mitochondria and rough endoplasmic reticulum.

The presence of ribosomes at these contact sites led me to pursue my postdoctoral studies in Maria Barna's lab at Stanford University, where I combined my background in chemical biology, methods development, and proteomics with training in RNA, molecular, cell, and developmental biology to establish proteomic workflows to characterize ribosome composition. Specifically, I characterized ribosome composition at the level of ribosome-associated proteins (RAPs) and ribosomal protein post-translational modifications in embryonic stem cells and tissues to understand how the role of RAPs and ribosomal protein post-translational modifications in translational control of gene expression, cell differentiation, and metabolism.
